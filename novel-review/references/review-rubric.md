# Novel review rubric

## Blocking dimensions

- User requirement: requested scope, format, length, viewpoint, content boundary, and protected elements.
- Continuity: time, place, sequence, prior events, objects, injuries, and knowledge.
- Canon: CREATOR.md, stable lore, world rules, ability limits, and established relationships.
- Character logic: motivation, decision pressure, knowledge, voice, and behavioral consistency.
- Plot logic: causality, stakes, information flow, consequences, and unresolved promises.

For action-heavy or procedure-heavy scenes, explicitly check:

- who performs each action and who owns the current object, tool, or resource;
- where important objects are before and after the action;
- whether time, distance, physical condition, rules, and device procedures permit the transition;
- whether an action, result, or prop state actually supports the next line of dialogue.

## Advisory dimensions

- Pacing: scene speed, repetition, weak escalation, or abrupt transitions.
- Prose: vague wording, over-explanation, repetitive sentence patterns, or summary replacing action.
- Dialogue: identical voices, unnatural exposition, or missing response pressure.
- Viewpoint: unexplained knowledge or detached analysis in a limited viewpoint.
- Style: violations of confirmed project style or formatting rules.
- AI-pattern observation: generic abstraction, formulaic transitions, excessive symmetrical phrasing, or repeated conclusions.
- Scene shape: an opening that omits the minimum event context, a suspense beat that explains its cause before establishing the anomaly or result, or an ending that does not point to a concrete next action or answerable question.
- Serialized pacing: stalled active threads, repeated low-impact chapters, or a new thread introduced without a role in the current escalation. Treat fast/stable/new line labels and payoff-frequency targets as adjustable editorial heuristics, never as universal laws.

For dialogue, check turn by turn:

- each question or statement responds to the preceding action, information, or intent;
- deliberate evasion is made visible through a pause, action, distance change, or viewpoint inference;
- gestures and reactions carry scene meaning instead of being inserted only to break up quotation marks.

## Severity

- blocker: the chapter cannot be accepted without repair.
- major: a material continuity, logic, character, or user-requirement problem.
- minor: a localized issue with limited impact.
- advisory: an optional improvement that does not block acceptance.

## Evidence rule

Every finding needs a path and line, paragraph, or distinctive phrase. State the source used to prove a continuity or canon claim. Mark uncertainty instead of presenting a guess as fact.
